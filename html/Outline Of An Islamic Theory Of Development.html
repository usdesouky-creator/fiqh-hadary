Outline of an Islamic Theory of Development

د. سيد دسوقي حسن

The seven elements
  1. 1. Goals of development
  2. 2. Main parameters of development
  3. 3. Constraints of development
  4. 4. Spectrum of development
  5. 5. Mechanisms of development
  6. 6. Clash of developments
  7. 7. Manpower for the management of development

The skeleton of our Islamic theory of development consists of seven elements:

  1. 1. Goals of development in Islam: food against hunger; security against fear.
  2. 2. Main parameters of development: function, benefit, beauty; size, national experience, extension in time and space.
  3. 3. Constraints of development: on the individual, family, societal, international and environmental levels.
  4. 4. Spectrum of development: survival development (تنمية البقاء), growth development (تنمية النماء) and competitive development (تنمية السبق).
  5. 5. Mechanisms of development: on the individual, societal and governmental levels.
  6. 6. Clash of developments, or clash of civilizations: the need for protection, since there is a war waged against our direction in development.
  7. 7. Manpower for the management of development (from philosophy to field): philosophers, planners, system analysts and designers, educators, trainers and evaluators.

1. Goals of development

  • Feeding (from) hunger.
  • Securing (from) fear.

﴿الَّذِي أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَآمَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍ﴾ [Quraysh 106:4]

The wording of the Quran for these goals also carries within it the nature of the development to be designed. Feeding is not out of a state of pure indulgence in pleasure (taraf), but out of a state of hunger. Securing is not out of a state of complete unconsciousness (ghafla), but out of a state of legitimate fear. It is a mirror replica of another Quranic law:

﴿فَإِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا * إِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا * فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَانصَبْ * وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَارْغَب﴾ [al-Sharh 94:5–8]

With every hardship comes ease. With every hardship comes ease. Hence, when you are freed (from distress), remain steadfast, and unto your Sustainer turn with love.

Fear and hunger represent the continuous hardship that man has to face, and man has to release whatever stresses keep him tense through the process of securing and feeding. When you are relieved of whatever is stressing you, restart toiling towards your God with love.

2. Main parameters of development

We measure the state of development by the following parameters:

Benefitالنفعية
Sizeالحجم
National experienceالخبرة الوطنية
Extension in time and spaceالامتداد الزماني والمكاني

3. Constraints of development

3.1 On the individual level

Preservation of the humanity of man. Man is not a slave of development. Man should be freed from all obstacles that stand in his way. He has to use his intellect to the maximum, guided by ethical values and driven by his creed.

3.2 On the family level

Maximization of family ties. Roles are a function of the nature of every member of the family: child, woman, man.

3.3 On the societal level

  • Society should proceed in development by forming professional organizations.
  • Innovation that leads to moral decadence should be abandoned.

3.4 On the international level

  • Every nation has the right to its own identity in the kind of development it chooses.
  • Multiplicity of development models is a fact of life. The tendency of one model to dominate the whole earth should be rejected.

3.5 On the environmental level

  • Every technology that leads to the pollution of our mother earth should be abandoned. Our environment is not only for us; it is also for the generations to come.
  • Modern technology has proven disastrous to our environment. Something has to be done.

4. Spectrum of development

Our theory is based on the concept of multiple-scale development. To start, we might consider only three scales:

Survivalتنمية البقاء
Growthتنمية النماء
Competitionتنمية السبق

Survival development

  • Local materials.
  • Local experience.
  • Basically local marketing.
  • Highly tuned to the environment.
  • Decentralized: no state interference.
  • Requires simple infrastructure.
  • Examples: dairy, bread, housing, clothing, vegetable drying, meat, food, natural medicine, water and farming, transportation, energy.

Growth development

  • Local materials.
  • Advanced experience based on modern science and technology. The use of science and technology needs a great deal of research to develop new, simple techniques to replace the old ones, as well as new products.
  • Basically satisfying the local market.
  • Tuned to the environment.
  • More complicated infrastructure.
  • More centralized.
  • Example: the wood industry in Egypt.

Competitive development

  • Advanced science and technology, and extensive research and development.
  • International materials.
  • Foreign and local know-how.
  • Modern infrastructure.
  • Centralization.
  • International market and severe competition.
  • Should be restricted to strategic areas according to priorities. That is to say, we should not spend the resources allocated to this area on things that can be achieved by survival and growth development.
  • Examples: aerospace industries, automotive industries.

Without R&D, the depth of industries decreases with time; even the depth of maintenance diminishes with time. In the fifties and sixties in Egypt, the depth of the automotive industry used to be high; in the nineties it was less than it had been in the forties.

5. Mechanisms of development

5.1 On the individual level

  • Readiness to give out of what God has bestowed upon you.
  • This giving should always be governed by God-consciousness, so that it is always directed to the best purposes by the best means.
  • Man has to be patient, not waiting for the results of his giving. He should believe that God will reward him in this life and in the life to come. The fruit of his good work cannot be but a beautiful reward.

5.2 On the societal level

  • Society should structure itself into functional groups. Every group is to carry the burden of fulfilling a certain need of society.
  • In the old times this used to be the tribal structure. Today it will be essentially the professional structure.
  • Of course, these structures will form along contours of social similarity. In the absence of any societal structure, dictatorship and tyranny will prevail.
  • Of course, these inner structures should all cooperate and live in harmony with minimum clashes.
  • Unless we design a good structure for our societies, a societal death will occur.

The Muslim society is urged to form two kinds of institutions:

  1. 1. Institutions for urging people to seek ways and means of feeding the needy (مؤسسات الحض على إطعام المسكين).
  2. 2. Institutions that offer assistance to those who need it (مؤسسات الماعون).

In Surat al-Ma'un (Assistance) the whole concept is depicted clearly:

Hast thou ever considered (the kind of man) who gives the lie to all moral law? Behold, it is this (kind of man) that thrusts the orphan away, and feels no urge to feed the needy. Woe, then, unto those praying ones whose hearts from their prayers are remote; those who want only to be seen and praised, and, withal, deny all assistance (to their fellow men).

[al-Ma'un 107:1–7]

Muslims translated the idea of ma'un into a very powerful social institution, namely the waqf system.

  • Waqf is basically a developmental tool that characterized our life, even in the lowest moments of our history.
  • Most of our revolutionary governments shrank the waqf system almost to nothing.
  • Muslims should devise new methods to initiate what I call a developmental waqf.
  • One of the missing links in our present life is a waqf institution for the promotion of innovation. A preliminary structure for this institution was studied by the scientific committee of the Egyptian Syndicate of Engineers, and the results of these studies are available.

5.3 On the governmental level

The role of government in development should be concentrated on maximizing the freedom of society to interact with its national environment in making its suitable living, governed only by its creed, the Sharia and the prevailing systems. To translate this into definite directions, we might say:

  • The government should protect the national resources and all the survival technologies that have accumulated in our national heritage.
  • The government should help form different bodies of professionals working in different directions of development.
  • The government should urge people to spend on preserving and promoting their education and training systems, and it should build exemplary education systems.
  • All the needed infrastructure should be planned and initiated by the government.
  • Fostering certain technologies in their infancy is a duty of government.
  • The legal system should not be a burden on development.
  • International relations should aim at opening markets to national products.

6. Clash of developments

  • The big powers are trying to impose their ensemble of products on the globe.
  • Through a huge machinery of mass media they try to open markets.
  • They allow the release of only parts of minor technologies, just enough to keep the markets open.
  • They prevent the release of any strategic technology, and allow only technologies that can replace survival and growth technologies.
  • What we can do is to increase to the maximum the sectors that depend on survival and growth development.
  • At the same time, we should restrict our competitive development to the strategic industries, mainly aerospace technologies.
  • I suggest the initiation of an organization to foster and develop innovation in the most advanced areas.

7. Manpower for the management of development

From philosophy to field. There is a spectrum of people who have to participate in the development process:

  • We start with philosophers to give us the goals and nature of development. In every direction of development we must have an inner community that tells us what our creed and Sharia ask us to do. This is a Quranic order:

    ﴿فَلَوْلَا نَفَرَ مِن كُلِّ فِرْقَةٍ مِّنْهُمْ طَائِفَةٌ لِّيَتَفَقَّهُوا فِي الدِّينِ وَلِيُنذِرُوا قَوْمَهُمْ إِذَا رَجَعُوا إِلَيْهِمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَحْذَرُونَ﴾ [al-Tawba 9:122]

  • The philosophy will be turned into a plan by the planners.
  • The plan will be converted into a system by the system designer, and checked for good performance by the system analyst.
  • To realize the system we need education and training systems, so we must have educators and trainers (the idea of īlāf, إيلاف).
  • Finally, we must always measure our performance, compare the output with the goals, evaluate the system, and feed the results back to the planners to devise corrections. So we need evaluators.
